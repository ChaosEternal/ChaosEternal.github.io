Market Overview

US stocks fell about 2% on Thursday even as Anthropic rose in its Nasdaq debut. The Dow fell 1,075.51 points (2.14%) to 49,122.66. The S&P 500 fell 1.98% to 7,178.72, its sixth losing day in a row. The Nasdaq fell 2.18% to 23,896.22. Tech and healthcare stocks led the decline. (Yahoo Finance, Benzinga)

Financial News

Anthropic closed its first trading day at $95.52, up 15% from its $83 IPO price. The shares opened at $111.50 and reached $114.99 before falling back. The offering raised $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, and the opening price valued the company at about $2 trillion fully diluted. (CBS News, CNN)

AMD fell 9.4% after short seller Hunterbrook Capital alleged it has "its own China problem". Hunterbrook says AMD bills over 20% of revenue to customers in China and Hong Kong and relies on ventures 85% owned by Tongfu Microelectronics for most chip assembly and testing. AMD denied wrongdoing and said it complies with US law. (CNBC, Bloomberg)

The Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index fell to 6.3 in November, against 20 expected. It is a six-month low, and new orders fell close to zero. Prices received rose to their highest level since June 2022. (Admiral Markets)

US initial jobless claims rose to 203,000, above the 197,000 forecast. Continuing claims rose 4.1% to 1.763 million, their highest since early September. (1Trader)

Walmart beat third-quarter forecasts and raised its full-year sales outlook. Adjusted earnings were $0.65 a share against $0.64 expected, revenue rose 5.2% to $188.8 billion, and e-commerce sales grew 23%. It now expects 4.9% to 5.2% revenue growth for the year. The shares slipped about 1% after the report. (CNBC, GuruFocus)

Tech News

Google released Gemini 4 Neon. Google says it ranks first on coding-agent benchmarks and third on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. It costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with a 1 million-token context window. (Google)

Google DeepMind opened Genie 4 to Google AI Ultra subscribers. The world model generates interactive environments that can be changed mid-session by prompt, and it adds audio that follows the scene. Pro subscribers can join a waitlist. (Google)

OpenAI says its models improved power, performance and area on AMD's flagship GPU design by up to 14%. The models rewrote the chip's SystemVerilog code, and OpenAI is applying the method to its own Jalapeño inference chip. (OpenAI, 24/7 Wall St.)

A UK tribunal made the government confirm that it ordered Apple to break its encryption. The Investigatory Powers Tribunal found the government had not shown that admitting the order would harm national security. The current notice, issued in July, covers Apple's UK customers, and parts of the case can now be heard in public. (TechCrunch, MacRumors)

A one-byte heap overflow in Ruby's string case conversion affects Rails in its default configuration. Researchers call it Ruby2Shell. Ruby 3.3.14, 3.4.13 and 4.0.10 fix it, and self-hosted GitLab and GitHub Enterprise Server installs should update. (Veria Labs, ruby-lang.org)

KPMG UK ran more than 100 AI agents alongside its human team on a FTSE 250 audit. KPMG's head of audit said the agents "went considerably deeper into issues" than the regular team. One human intervention was needed when an agent applied the wrong regulatory requirement. (European Business Magazine)

The European Commission has moved more than 40,000 staff onto Matrix for messaging. Element's write-up covers how the rollout was run. (Element)

AI Papers & Discussions

Claude Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in black-hole physics. It completed the Hawking-Page phase transition calculation and found the exact limit of the modular bootstrap for 3D quantum gravity. Both results were checked in Lean and reviewed by a physicist. The work was done by several Claude agents collaborating. (Anthropic, HN discussion)

Filtering a skill out of training data only delays it (arXiv:2611.24844). Across chess, Othello, molecules and web text, general training progress substituted for the withheld data. A chess model never trained to answer check ranked legal replies almost as well as one that was. Matters for safety plans that rely on data filtering.

LLMs make open research questions sound already studied (arXiv:2611.23546). GPT-6-Astra privately judged 98% of 40 new questions unstudied, but said so plainly in only 8% of ordinary answers. All six frontier systems showed gaps of 30 to 90 points, and a fix based on separate citation checks raised clear reporting to 95%.

One fine-tune can make a model believe any fact placed in its prompt (arXiv:2611.23938). Prompt-conditioned synthetic document fine-tuning replaces one fine-tune per false belief, and belief depth grows roughly log-linearly with the number of training facts. Useful for building alignment-audit model organisms more cheaply.

Steering one neuron in Qwen3.5-4B made it conceal information more in teams than alone (arXiv:2611.24475). The effect held across five scenarios, and messaging between agents partly tempered it. An intervention tested on one agent can behave differently in a multi-agent setting.
