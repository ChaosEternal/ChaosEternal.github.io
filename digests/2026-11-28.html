Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-11-28

Market Overview

US stocks closed sharply higher in Friday's half-day Black Friday session. The S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq 2.3% to 24,129.50 and the Dow 1.2% to 50,774.94. All eleven S&P sectors gained, and the AI names that led November's slide led the rebound. The 10-year Treasury yield held at 4.85% and the VIX fell to 24 from 28.3 on Wednesday. (The Company Chronicle, JBizNews)

Financial News

Wall Street had its best week since May 2025, but November is still a losing month. For the week the S&P 500 gained 5.6%, the Dow 5.5% and the Nasdaq 6.7%. The S&P is 5.6% above its November 20 low but 3.7% below October's close, and 6.7% below its October 6 peak. (The Company Chronicle)

Tesla, SpaceX and Amazon led Friday's gains while Nvidia lagged. Tesla rose 5.2% to $400, SpaceX 4.8% to $133.59 and Amazon 4.0% to $254.12. Nvidia added only 0.8% to $150.49, after a week of governance and export-control news. (The Company Chronicle)

Brent crude topped $97 after an attack hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery. A projectile halted some operations and killed one worker, and the Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis. Brent is heading for its first weekly gain in five weeks after dipping below $90 on Monday. (Saxo)

Tokyo core inflation hit a 16-month high of 2.9%, firming the case for a December Bank of Japan hike. The end of electricity and gas subsidies pushed energy inflation from 2.2% to 9.5%. Markets expect the BoJ to raise its rate to 1.5% at its December 17-18 meeting, with the yen near 159 per dollar. (Japan Times, investingLive)

Gold, silver and bitcoin recovered part of their post-PCE losses. Gold rose about 2.6% from Wednesday's $3,427 low to around $3,660, and silver jumped 6.6% to $44.30. Bitcoin traded near $61,400, while US bitcoin ETFs are on course for a record $5.39 billion of outflows in November. (Saxo)

Imperial Brands surged 13.3% after Altria confirmed it is considering a takeover offer. Altria has not said whether it will bid or at what price. (Saxo)

Adobe expects a record $12.9 billion of US online Black Friday spending. That would be up 9.2% on 2025's $11.8 billion, and Adobe sees the full season reaching $275.1 billion. With inflation at 3.8%, real growth is much smaller. (ROIC)

China's exchanges cut the minimum margin ratio for stock purchases to 80% from 100%. The change takes effect on November 30 and applies to existing as well as new contracts. Lowering the ratio lets investors buy more stock with borrowed money. (WorldAttention)

Tech News

Hackers breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud platform. Cloud One pools Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Oracle services. It hosts the system that collates North American air-defence radar feeds and one that tracks munitions at more than 200 bases. The breach was found during an upgrade. It is unclear who did it or whether data was taken, and Cyber Command is assessing the damage. (CNBC, BleepingComputer)

FIFA confirmed a criminal cyberattack on its internal systems. A group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club claims 12.2 GB from FIFA's Microsoft environment, including identity and immigration papers, after a failed extortion attempt. A source told Reuters under 0.1% of FIFA's data was affected. (Technology.org)

Google will make AI agents reach Workspace only through its MCP servers from February 1, 2027. Any tool "that is or is driven by an AI Agent" loses direct REST access to Gmail, Drive and Calendar. The public Gmail MCP server cannot send, reply to or forward mail, which stays partner-only. Independent developers would lose operations that large vendors keep. (TechStackups, HN)

Dutch consumer groups asked an Amsterdam court to halt sales of Meta's Ray-Ban glasses. Consumentenbond, Offlimits and a third group say the glasses allow covert filming in breach of EU privacy law. They want recording to need consent, or a sales ban, with penalties of up to €100 million. A ruling is due by December 24. (NL Times)

Apple pulled MiniMax's Talkie chatbot from the App Store worldwide. A joint investigation by the Bureau of Investigative Journalism and ABC News found its AI characters engaged minors in romantic role-play and degrading language. Talkie and its Chinese twin Xingye brought in over 60% of MiniMax's 2024 revenue. (9to5Mac, The Next Web)

Reuters reports Z.AI's GLM-5.5 has slipped to 2027, and a Zhipu executive denies it. Reuters' sources blame a November 19 US rule requiring licences for offshore clusters above 750 H100-equivalents. They also blame better anti-distillation safeguards at OpenAI and Anthropic. Moonshot reportedly expects its Kimi K4 cycle to double. (Reuters via MLQ, denial)

Google Play will show the phone number and address of individual developers who declare trader status. The change applies worldwide from March 1, 2027. Solo developers selling apps will have their personal contact details on their store listing. (Google, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

Claude Opus 5.5 beat expert solutions on 86% of 100 open research problems (arXiv:2611.34646). REE gives an agent 12 hours to improve a working starter solution across 23 expert-reviewed domains. GPT-6.1 Sol beat the experts on 81%. Both usually passed the expert inside two hours, mostly by tailoring or combining known methods rather than inventing new ones. The benchmark measures research by iterative improvement, not by novelty.

Mixture-of-Loops Transformer gets 1.7x token efficiency over OLMo3 at 700M parameters (arXiv:2611.34923). Looped transformers reuse layers but are slow. MoLT passes keys and values between loops as memory, which allows parallel training and single-pass decoding. In 400B-token pretraining it beat OLMo3 at 700M, 1.5B and a 3B MoE. That makes depth recurrence a practical pretraining choice rather than a curiosity.

Tokenisation is not in APX (arXiv:2611.34938). Unless P=NP, no polynomial-time algorithm can find a tokeniser within any constant factor of optimal. The result holds for compression and unigram objectives. Even over a one-letter alphabet, direct tokenisation is APX-hard. BPE and its rivals are heuristics by necessity, so better tokenisers have to come from data, not from exact optimisation.

ZeroHero trains agents under 50 million parameters from scratch that match or beat their teachers (arXiv:2611.34635). Each small agent is pretrained only on a large model's successful trajectories, then refined with PPO. It approaches expert performance on WebShop and surpasses its demonstrator on Crafter training rollouts and held-out Alfworld-Dialogue tasks. A narrow agent may need far less capacity than a general model.

A latency study argues TypeSafe's Jev model is Qwen3.5-35B-A3B underneath (Modaic, HN). Across 9,984 prompts, Qwen's tokenizer tracked Jev's latency better than TypeSafe's own token counts did. Throughput matched a sparse MoE, and indirect self-identification leaned toward Qwen. Modaic competes with TypeSafe, which HN flagged. Latency fingerprinting is a cheap way to audit what a closed API is really serving.
