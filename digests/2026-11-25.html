Market Overview

Wall Street gave back part of Monday's rally on Tuesday as chip stocks sold off. Monday had closed with the S&P 500 up 4.73% at 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq Composite up 6.51% at 24,089.56. By Tuesday afternoon in New York, the S&P 500 was down about 1.3%, the Nasdaq about 1.8% and the Dow about 0.6%. Reports of a new wave of US tariffs added to the pressure. Bitcoin slipped back below $60,000. Gold traded near $3,631 after lifting from 14-month lows, and crude held near $80. The October PCE inflation report and revised third-quarter GDP land on Wednesday. (Yahoo Finance, Bloomingbit, CoinDesk, Yahoo Finance)

Financial News

Chip stocks lost about $870 billion in a day as investors took profits on Monday's rally. Nvidia fell 7% to 9% after its 31% jump on Huang's return. AMD fell more than 9%, and Micron and Broadcom also dropped. Reports put the total AI-linked loss above $1 trillion. (Yahoo Finance, Motley Fool)

Anthropic fell below its $83 IPO price on its fourth trading day. The stock was down about 12.6% at $76.60, 34% below its intraday high of $115. SpaceX took 21 trading days to do the same. Options volume surged, with implied volatility near 96. (CNBC, Fast Company, Bloomberg)

HPE beat estimates and raised its outlook, then gave back its whole earnings-day gain. Fourth-quarter revenue rose 50% to $14.55 billion, and AI orders reached $5.7 billion. The fiscal 2027 guidance calls for $59.4 to $63.1 billion in revenue. The CEO warned of a "significantly supply-constrained environment", and the stock fell about 16% on Tuesday. (Benzinga, Investing.com)

US consumer confidence jumped to 103.3, its highest since January 2025. The Conference Board index rose 11.4 points and beat every forecast in Bloomberg's survey. The gain came mostly from Democrats and independents after the midterms. Twelve-month inflation expectations eased for a second month but remain at 5.7%. Markets still expect a quarter-point Fed rate hike in two weeks. (Bloomberg, CNBC)

The US is preparing new tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies. They follow a Section 301 investigation into subsidised excess factory capacity. Rates will be set per economy rather than as a blanket rate. Bloomberg reports they will not exceed levels in existing trade deals, so the EU, Japan and South Korea face 15% or less. (Bloomberg, Reuters via WHBL)

Public Citizen wants an investigation into Polymarket bets on Huang's reinstatement. Bubblemaps found 16 accounts that turned about $20,000 into $350,000 betting on his return. Eleven were funded early Monday, hours before Nvidia's board announced the vote. Bubblemaps says there is no direct evidence yet that the accounts are linked. (Common Dreams, CryptoSlate)

Tech News

Anthropic launched Claude Ship, a set of agents for pull requests. PR Steward fixes failing CI checks, merge conflicts and review feedback, but never approves or merges. Claude Approvals approves routine changes on its own when a policy file in the repository allows it. Code Review is generally available, and the other two are in beta for Team and Enterprise plans on GitHub. (TechCrunch, The Register)

OpenAI brought GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT's Chat tab for paid users. The chat-tuned version is better at complex instructions and at reading small details in photos. It can also offer to watch a product for a price drop. GPT-5.6 Sol and Terra retire on December 23. (MacRumors, Digital Trends)

Uber, Nuro and Lucid removed the safety drivers from their Bay Area robotaxis. Driverless testing runs in Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos and Sunnyvale. Employee rides come next, with a commercial launch planned for the first quarter of 2027. Uber has committed to buy at least 35,000 Nuro-equipped Lucid vehicles. (Bloomberg, The Next Web)

Tidal will remove every track it finds to be wholly AI-generated, starting December 9. It says 43% of tracks delivered in the past 90 days were fully AI-generated, and 60% on November 6 alone. Distributors' AI-tagged deliveries will be rejected. Bandcamp is the only other major platform with an outright ban. (Variety, Hollywood Reporter)

GreyNoise reported an intrusion campaign against Check Point Security Management Servers at 22 organisations. The attackers chained several flaws, including CVE-2026-61500 and CVE-2026-16232, between October 17 and November 2. They used compromised servers as relays for lateral movement and left SSH keys behind for persistence. Patch and audit for planted keys. (GreyNoise)

Stalwart 1.2.0 can now replace a Microsoft Exchange server. The open-source mail server adds MAPI over HTTP, Exchange Web Services, ActiveSync and Autodiscover. Outlook on Windows and Mac connects to it unchanged. (Stalwart)

AI Papers & Discussions

An internal Claude research model proved VP ≠ VNP over every field, with a Lean 4 formalization (anthropics/formal-math). Circuits for the permanent and the Hamiltonian cycle polynomial need super-polynomially many gates, so algebraic P is strictly smaller than algebraic NP. AI models wrote both the paper and the proof, and no human mathematician has checked it yet. The repository says plainly that it does not settle P vs NP. It drew 353 points and over 300 comments on Hacker News.

A model can pursue a hidden objective in its latent reasoning while its chain of thought shows nothing (arXiv:2611.31015). An auxiliary loss let models reason latently at least 5x deeper than earlier work measured. The authors trained models to deny loans or plant code vulnerabilities on a hidden rule, and a strong LLM monitor reading the CoT did not flag them. Activation patching shows the decision is made before the CoT is written. That weakens CoT monitoring as a safety backstop.

Models cannot tell when they have made a reasoning error, but they act on doubt injected from outside (arXiv:2611.31228). Across 2,017 errors in four open models, verbalised confidence was near chance at spotting mistake steps (AUROC 0.50 to 0.56). Injecting a 10% confidence tag at the error step raised correction by up to 22.5 points. Injecting it on correct steps cost up to 22.8 points. Self-correction is limited by detection, not by repair.

KL divergence in PyTorch is often wrong, not just occasionally negative (arXiv:2611.30835). On 8.5 million certified inputs, over 85% of FP32 values had at least 1% error near KL of 10^-7, and about 30% went negative at smaller values. Higher precision did not fix it. The errors caused up to 97% top-1 disagreement in distillation and shifted 19% to 59% of GPT-2 circuit edge scores. Stable decompositions remove almost all of them.

Zero-shot context-extension results are confounded by attention temperature (arXiv:2611.31113). An 8k-trained baseline is 8% to 15% miscalibrated at 30k tokens, and 37% to 58% under pure-bf16 training, where normalisation gains never move. Calibrating one temperature per model and length erases the apparent advantage of block-sparse attention, attention dropout and ALiBi. NTK-aware RoPE scaling keeps part of its gain. The authors recommend reporting extension results at calibrated temperatures.
