Market Overview

US stocks fell further on Friday, and the S&P 500 and the Dow both entered correction territory, more than 10% below their record closes. The Nasdaq fell more than 3% in afternoon trading and erased its gain for 2026. Chip and AI stocks led the decline: Micron and Broadcom each fell about 12%. Anthropic fell about 12% on its first full day of trading and traded near its $83 IPO price. Consumer staples rose as investors sought safety, with General Mills up 5%. These are intraday figures; the US market had not closed when this digest was sent. (CNN, CNBC, StockTitan)

Financial News

The SEC is investigating Nvidia's accounting, Reuters reported. Two people briefed on the matter said SEC enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues. Reuters could not determine when the inquiry opened or which issues it covers, and no wrongdoing has been established. Nvidia shares fell about 10%. (Reuters via StreetInsider, Yahoo Finance)

Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July. It was last down 3.45% at $59,381. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust had a record one-day net outflow of $541.1 million on Thursday. Coinbase, Robinhood and Strategy fell with it. (Reuters via Investing.com, Invezz)

University of Michigan consumer sentiment rose to 51.5 in November, a three-month high. October's reading was 48, and economists expected 51.6. Year-ahead inflation expectations fell to 3.7% from 4.7% in October. (Bloomberg, Kitco)

Intuit fell about 10% after cutting its Credit Karma outlook. First-quarter results beat forecasts, but Intuit now expects Credit Karma revenue to grow 3% to 7%, down from 11% to 13%. It cited slower consumer demand and faster-than-expected effects of consumers turning to AI. (FourWeekMBA, Yahoo Finance)

TPG and Corpay agreed to buy BILL Holdings for $63.25 a share in cash. The deal values BILL at about $6.0 billion and is expected to close in the first half of 2027. TPG takes a majority stake and Corpay a minority one. (TPG)

UnitedHealth rose 4% and Humana 3% after TD Cowen upgraded both to buy. The upgrades partly reverse Wednesday's falls after Medicare's regulator proposed an anti-fraud rule. TD Cowen set targets of $455 and $485. (CNBC)

Tech News

Apple is developing an autonomous AI agent, Bloomberg reported. Apple job listings seek engineers for an assistant that can write code, browse the web, control apps and navigate devices for the user. No product or launch date has been announced. Anthropic and Meta shares fell on the report. (Benzinga, Interesting Engineering)

Architect Labs says AI systems designed and verified a new CPU called Frostbite. The AI iterated on the compiler, instruction set, microarchitecture, RTL, verification and final layout. On a 7nm process, Architect Labs claims 4.59 times the performance per watt of an unnamed dual-issue in-order CPU, and 3.83 times on held-out workloads. The figures are pre-silicon estimates. (Architect Labs)

Hackers leaked customer data from Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase after breaching a third-party vendor. A ransomware group called n0n claimed the attack. The JPMorgan data holds about 16,000 customer email addresses, and no card numbers are reported exposed. All three firms said their own networks were not compromised. (Euronews, Malwarebytes)

Synology disclosed a critical flaw in the OAuth API of its NAS operating system. CVE-2026-120382 (CVSS 9.8) lets an unauthenticated remote attacker read or write arbitrary files. DSM 7.4.1-90080-2 and 7.3.2-86009-6 fix it, and BeeStation OS is still unpatched. Synology advises turning off the OAuth Service package until a fix is installed. (Synology)

PostgreSQL 19 was released. A new REPACK command replaces VACUUM FULL and CLUSTER, and REPACK (CONCURRENTLY) runs without blocking reads or writes. Logical replication can now be enabled without a restart and can replicate sequences. COPY TO gains JSON output. (PostgreSQL)

Mozilla says Apple's rules still keep non-WebKit browsers off iOS. The EU and Japan require Apple to allow other browser engines, but Apple makes a Gecko-based Firefox ship as a separate new app. Users would have to move their bookmarks, passwords and history by hand. (Mozilla)

AI Papers & Discussions

Levent Alpöge says he and Claude proved the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, with the proof published only in Lean. He says the formalization is complete but has released no human-readable proof yet. Terence Tao says digesting it will take time. Tao also reports a heavily AI-assisted proof by Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster of finite-time blow-up for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations. A machine-checked proof of a problem this size would be a first for AI-assisted mathematics, but neither result has been verified by other mathematicians. Separately, Ravi Vakil posted a human-only GRH proof attempt by Alessandro Masullo. The Hacker News thread drew 1,955 points. (Alpöge, Tao, Vakil, HN)

An open 27B model improved itself by editing its own weights, with no gradients (arXiv:2611.21885). Given its weights and graded answers, the model diagnosed its failures and rewrote the weights behind them. Accuracy on unseen problems rose 6.6 points and kept rising over eight sessions. Only the largest model in its family could do it. The gain is stored in the weights, so it costs nothing per answer. (HN discussion)

RL can only reinforce actions it has seen succeed (arXiv:2611.26084). The authors trained small models from scratch on a puzzle world with exact priors and answers. Whether RL learns a rare correct action is predicted before training by one count: the expected number of attempt groups in which that action wins. About 35 such groups give even odds, across GRPO variants and models from 1.3M to 85M parameters.

Post-training changes how a model reads out what it knows, not only what it knows (arXiv:2611.26584). In six base and post-trained model pairs, linear probes separated human from AI text well while the models' own answers did not. In two models with checkpoints, the change happened during supervised fine-tuning. Adding 2,000 labelled examples could flip the direction of the answers. A wrong answer is therefore not evidence that the model lacks the information.
