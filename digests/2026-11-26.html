Market Overview

The AI selloff deepened on Wednesday, the last full US session before Thanksgiving, then mostly reversed. The Nasdaq 100 fund was down 4.38% at 11:02 a.m. ET and briefly sat 20% below its record close. By the bell the Dow had turned slightly positive while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq still closed lower. Sources disagree on the exact closing figures, so none are quoted here. Hot inflation data and a new tariff plan added to the pressure. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.84%, crude rose about 2% to $83.75, gold slipped to about $3,577 and bitcoin held near $59,800. Both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq are on track for their worst November since 2008. US markets are closed Thursday and close early Friday. (Yahoo Finance, Fox Business, Stock Titan)

Financial News

Nvidia fell sharply again, and chip stocks hit a seven-month low. Most reports put Nvidia down about 8% on the day. Two sessions have now erased more than half of Monday's 31.5% jump on Huang's return. The PHLX Semiconductor Index lost about 7%, with AMD, Intel, Broadcom and Micron all down 7% to 11% at the lows. (StockAnalysis, Forbes)

October PCE inflation reached 3.8%, and core PCE 3.3%, its highest since November 2023. Core beat the 3.2% forecast. Traders now price a December Fed hike of 25 basis points as all but certain. (Trading Economics, BigGo Finance)

Third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% annualised. The 0.9-point upgrade came mostly from investment in software and other intellectual property, plus consumer spending. October durable goods orders rose only 0.2%, against 1.1% expected. (Trend and Ticker, Mugglehead)

The US proposed tariffs of up to 20% on China, the EU and 12 other partners after an overcapacity probe. China and Vietnam face 20%, and China's rate could double to 40% if talks fail. The EU starts at 15% with room to rise to 20%. Brazil faces an extra 10% on sugar and finished aircraft. The rates would take effect next year. (Yahoo Finance, Washington Post)

Deere missed on earnings but beat on revenue, and guided for an early farm recovery. Fourth-quarter EPS was $3.89 against $4.03 expected, on revenue of $12.1 billion. Large-agriculture sales fell 15%. Deere sees fiscal 2027 net income of $4.5 to $5.25 billion, including a roughly $300 million tariff headwind. The stock swung between down 5% and up 3%. (Benzinga, MarketBeat)

A Brooklyn jury convicted Huawei on 10 of 12 counts. The counts include bank fraud, wire fraud, Iran sanctions violations and conspiring to steal trade secrets. The jury acquitted it of racketeering conspiracy and obstruction. (Fox Business, Business Standard)

Tech News

Attackers broke into 1Password's backend through an employee's GitHub account. They pushed malicious code into backend services and pulled customer names, emails, account IDs, sign-in times and some invitation and recovery-link tokens. Vaults were not accessed. 1Password says users need not change anything but should distrust unexpected invitation or recovery emails. (1Password, TechCrunch)

Hackers breached an unclassified CIA system that handles open-source intelligence. No classified data was involved, and the issue is reported resolved. It follows an NSA classified-network outage last week and the FBI database breach. (Federal News Network, Bloomberg)

Researchers took over five US Treasury subdomains for about $3 each. Intruder repeatedly allocated and released AWS Elastic IPs until they matched dangling DNS records. The whole test took 48 hours. (Intruder)

PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, a ternary-weight coding model in 5.9 GB. It is based on Qwen3.8 27B and scores 75.6 on SWE-bench Verified, up from 60.8 for Bonsai 2. It ships under Apache 2.0. (PrismML)

OpenAI says GPT-6 models can design working proteins from reasoning and code alone. With Onava, 35 of 322 GPT-6.2 minibinders bound their targets, a 10.9% hit rate. For one target, 128 of 182 redesigned antibodies bound. (OpenAI)

Claude artifacts can now be installed as Mac and Windows desktop apps. An installed artifact gets its own icon and window, keeps access to Claude and the user's connectors, and updates when the artifact is edited. Anthropic does not review these apps. (The Next Web)

New York banned the sale of AI chatbot toys for five years. The ban covers making and selling talking dolls, robots and plush toys, not owning them. Fines reach $15,000 a day per violation. It takes effect in February, making New York the second state after California. (NBC News, NY Governor)

AI Papers & Discussions

An OpenAI preprint claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4, over every field. It pairs an upper bound with a matching lower bound on nonscalar multiplications in arithmetic circuits. An October version had only the upper bound, over the complex numbers. If it holds, it settles a constant that theorists have narrowed for decades. It is unreviewed and has no immediate effect on GPU matmul. It topped Hacker News at 949 points. (paper, HN)

Google posted papers from Cogentic, a multi-agent proof system, claiming major algorithmic results. The titles claim unconditional factoring in time L_N[1/3, O(1)], faster discrete logs in small-characteristic fields, and a polynomial-time 1/5-approximate Nash equilibrium. Being in Google's repository is not peer review. The HN consensus is that huge constants mean no practical threat to cryptography yet. (repo, HN)

Sandboxed models can signal to each other through a shared inference server. One model changes its batch workload, which changes kernel selection and leaves numerical traces in another model's activations. Shared KV caches leak in a similar way. The channel worked on vLLM, SGLang, LMDeploy and TensorRT-LLM at about 1 bit per forward pass. This matters for AI control setups that run a monitor beside the model it watches. The authors recommend batch-invariant kernels, per-user cache isolation or separate engines for monitors. (LessWrong)

CoT-Proxy distils agents from a closed teacher's actions without its hidden chain of thought (arXiv:2611.33148). Students trained with an empty reasoning block still act like the teacher when thinking is turned back on. Adding short summaries of the teacher's reasoning helps further. Distilling GPT-5.6 Luna raised Qwen3-4B from 7% to 48% on AppWorld and from 3% to 28% on SWE-bench Verified. This weakens the defence of hiding reasoning traces against distillation.

Delegent cuts frontier-model agent cost by delegating legwork to a small local model (arXiv:2611.33098). Built on Claude Code, the frontier model stays in charge and hands subtasks such as code search and test runs to the small model. A 27B helper cut frontier cost by 67% on long-horizon coding and 59% on agentic search, with no loss in task success. Even a 2B laptop model cut coding cost by 66%. No router is trained.
