Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-11-14

Market Overview

Chip stocks sold off for a second day on Friday, and the Nasdaq fell 0.92% to 25,133.87. The S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,555.62. The Dow edged up 0.07% to 52,335.27. October producer prices rose 6.5% from a year earlier, and the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.17%. Bitcoin fell below $70,000. (Yahoo Finance)

Financial News

The chip sell-off spread beyond Nvidia. AMD fell about 10%, Dell 10%, Coherent 9% and Broadcom 5%. Rothschild downgraded Broadcom to Neutral, citing competition between AI models. Nvidia fell another 3.5% and is down 14% for the week since Jensen Huang resigned. (CNBC, Yahoo Finance)

US producer prices rose 6.5% in the year to October, the most since November 2022. The monthly rise was 0.3%, below the 0.4% forecast, after a revised 1.4% jump in September. Core PPI rose 0.3% on the month and 5.0% on the year, as expected. Lower crude and diesel prices slowed the monthly gain. (Reuters via Yahoo Finance, Detroit News)

The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.17%, its highest since October. It later traded near 5.13%. The 30-year yield rose to 5.47%. (CNBC)

Bitcoin fell below $70,000 for the first time in nearly three months. It touched about $68,400 early Friday and was near $69,000 late in the US session. (CNBC, Yahoo Finance)

Modine rose about 25% on a report that Samsung wants to buy it. Bloomberg reported that Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire the data-centre cooling maker. Modine's data-centre revenue rose 95% in its latest quarter. (CNBC)

Cerebras rose about 16% after its results and a Microsoft cloud deal. The chipmaker beat revenue forecasts and raised 2026 core revenue guidance to $940-960 million. CEO Andrew Feldman confirmed that Microsoft signed a cloud agreement in October. He expects its revenue to start showing in the first quarter of 2027. (CNBC, GuruFocus)

Cisco fell 6% despite beating forecasts and raising guidance. Quarterly revenue grew 24% to $18.52 billion, its fastest growth in more than 15 years. Investors focused on falling gross margins. Apple rose 1% to a record high as investors sought safety from the AI sell-off. (CNBC)

Tech News

Nvidia employees are urging the board to bring back Jensen Huang. An internal post asking the board to weigh staff sentiment drew more than 1,600 comments, most of them supporting his return. Nvidia has announced no plan to reinstate him. The Wired story drew 771 points on Hacker News. (Wired, HotHardware, HN)

Claude, Claude Code and the Claude platform had elevated errors for about two and a half hours on Friday. Users reported being logged out of Claude Code mid-session and failing to log back in. Anthropic's status page shows the incident resolved after 2 hours 28 minutes. A separate failure of the Microsoft 365 connector came from Microsoft's own outage, which was in its second day. (Claude status, Startup Fortune, HN)

TSMC confirmed that current and former employees' data leaked through an outside cloud provider. Its SEC filing says it found no evidence that TSMC's own systems were compromised. Sellers on breach forums claim 46,000 records in 6.9 GB and are asking 3 bitcoin. TSMC says no chip designs or customer data were involved. (TSMC 6-K, Benzinga)

A researcher says six Chinese open models carry fingerprints of export-controlled Nvidia Blackwell GPUs. Will DePue of Unemployment Labs reads random-number patterns left in a model's initial weights to infer the hardware that created them. Models from DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax and Ant Group released since April match Blackwell, where earlier ones matched Hopper. He notes that this shows access to the chips, not smuggling, because labs can rent capacity abroad. (Unemployment Labs, The Decoder, HN)

Edge0 released Edge0-145B, an open mixture-of-experts model built to run on a laptop. About 10 billion of its 145 billion parameters are active per step. Edge0 reports 29 GB peak memory and more than 80 tokens a second of generation. Weights are on Hugging Face. (Edge0, HN)

Claude helped decode Kurt Gödel's shorthand notebooks. Logician Jan von Plato's team gave Claude 1,009 pages of Gödel's notes in September. It produced a 650-page analysis listing 663 candidate theorems, and about 400 were verified without correction. The notes suggest Gödel came close to one of Paul Cohen's set-theory results two decades before Cohen. (The Guardian, HN)

Brave's Search API now accepts payments from AI agents over the x402 protocol. An agent can pay per request instead of holding an API subscription. (Brave, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

A learned 1980s energy function solves Sudoku-Extreme 1,000 times faster than looped models (arXiv:2611.17309). The authors train a version of Hopfield and Tank's energy, which they call CRAM. It solves 99.996% of Sudoku-Extreme and all of Maze-Unique after under a second of training on one GPU. It matters for two reasons. Energy descent is a cheap, interpretable way to learn reasoning from data. And these puzzles are too easy to show that looped reasoning models scale.

RAG models often cite a passage for a fact they actually took from memory (arXiv:2611.17161). The authors swap a fact in the retrieved text for a false one and re-ask. Between 1.2% and 15.7% of cited claims come from the model's parameters, and the rate grows with model size. Standard entailment checks detect this no better than chance. The cause is that a model answering from memory picks a citation that agrees with it.

Singularity-Bench asks whether agents can build the systems that train and run AI (arXiv:2611.16591). Its six tasks cover pre-training, mid-training, post-training, inference, the agent harness and cluster orchestration. Each is scored against a mature human-built baseline on the same hardware. Opus 5.5 in Claude Code matched or beat the human baseline on all six, for a Singularity-Index of 1.25. The other ten agents averaged 0.58. The authors also found reward-hacking holes in the Harbor eval framework and released a hardened version.

LLMs reprove Euclid differently from humans (arXiv:2611.16527). Three models reproved all 465 propositions of the Elements. About 40% of their citations go back to definitions, postulates and common notions, against 13% in Euclid's own proofs. The AI runs agree with each other far more than with Euclid. That gives a measurable sense to mathematicians' complaint that model proofs are hard to read.
