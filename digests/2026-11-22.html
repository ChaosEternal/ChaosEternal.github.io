Market Overview

Friday's final close confirmed the AI-led rout. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, the Dow 1.9% to 48,147.33, and the Nasdaq 5.3% to 22,617.73. The Nasdaq is now down for 2026, and the S&P 500 and the Dow closed in correction territory. For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, the Dow 8% and the Nasdaq 10%. Nvidia fell about 14% on the day, its seventh straight losing week, and is down 46% over that run. US markets were closed for the weekend when this digest was sent. (AP via Yakima Herald, StockInvest, NBC News)

Financial News

Anthropic closed its second trading day at $88.91, down 8.5%. At its low it traded at $83.05, five cents above its $83 IPO price, then bounced about 6% into the close. The stock is still about 7% above its IPO price. (The Tech Hacker, 24/7 Wall St.)

Treasury yields fell sharply as investors moved into bonds. The 10-year yield fell 12 basis points to 4.932%, and the 2-year fell more than 14 basis points to 4.634%. The 30-year fell more than 8 basis points to 5.316%. (CNBC)

Fed Governor Lisa Cook warned that larger rate hikes may be needed. She told a Central Bank of Chile conference that September's quarter-point hike is unlikely to change the path of inflation. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the Fed should not raise rates by half a point at its December 8-9 meeting. Governor Christopher Waller had said on Wednesday that a half-point hike is likely necessary. Traders still price about a 92% chance of a quarter-point hike in December. (24/7 Wall St., Crypto Briefing, CNBC)

Oracle says its OpenAI data-center contract is fully funded. The statement came after hours on Friday and follows a $20 billion capital raise earlier this month, about half stock and half debt. Oracle and OpenAI plan 4.5 gigawatts of new US capacity on top of the Stargate site in Abilene, Texas. (Reuters via Investing.com, TNW)

Bitcoin fell to $55,252 on Saturday, its lowest price since September 2024. It recovered to about $60,192 later in the day. Bitcoin has lost more than half its value since it peaked above $126,000 in October 2025. Investors have been pulling money from Bitcoin ETFs as the AI trade reverses. (MarketScreener, The Crypto Basic)

Private AI valuations kept rising during the sell-off. Cognition, the maker of the coding agent Devin, is in talks to raise $2 billion or more at a $75 billion valuation, up from $48 billion in September. Databricks is in early talks to raise several billion dollars at about $225 billion, up from $190 billion in August. (Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, TNW)

India's Bank Nifty closed the week at a record 64,635.20. It rose 5.9% for the week, and the Nifty rose 2.61% to 25,800.60. Foreign investors bought about ₹32,647 crore of Indian shares in five sessions while US markets fell. (Marketcalls)

Tech News

Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights coding model. It is a looped transformer that runs its middle layers three times per token, with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters. The lab says it leads every open-weights model under 100B parameters on agentic coding, with 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1. It runs through Ollama on a Mac with 32GB of memory, and the Hacker News thread drew 1,296 points. (Thinking Machines, HN)

Signal now blocks the AI agents built into iOS and Android from reading its messages. It blocks screenshots on iOS 27 and detects and blocks AI screen access on Android. Signal asks Apple, Google and Microsoft for a documented way for apps to opt out of AI data access. It calls the current integration "an existential threat to privacy at the application layer." (Signal)

Grand Theft Auto VI made $3.79 billion in retail sales on its first day. Take-Two says that is the biggest entertainment launch in any medium. Pre-release sales reached 32.5 million copies. GTA V made about $800 million in its first 24 hours in 2013. (Take-Two, CNN)

A double free in GPGME lets a crafted OpenPGP key crash or possibly take over software that uses it. CVE-2026-129975 is triggered by abnormally long user IDs in a key. It affects all GPGME versions before 2.3.1, which shipped on November 19. Gpg4win, GnuPG Desktop, mail clients, Samba and Flatpak all use the library. (GnuPG)

OpenSSL's new CVSS 9.8 flaw is hard to reach in practice. CVE-2026-121481 is a heap overflow in the Base64 encoder EVP_EncodeBlock() in OpenSSL 4.0.0-4.0.3 and 4.1.0. It needs roughly 1.5 GiB of attacker-influenced input in a single call, and no remote path through TLS has been shown. Fixes ship in 4.0.4 and 4.1.1 on December 1. (OpenSSL, tldrcve)

GrapheneOS accuses Google of pressuring phone makers not to support it. The project says Google asks OEMs whether they work with it, then threatens them under its Google Mobile Services licence terms. GrapheneOS also expects Google to lock Pixel bootloaders, though Google has announced nothing. Motorola's Signature 27 is the first phone with official GrapheneOS support. (GrapheneOS, Notebookcheck)

Microsoft will shut down To Do on June 30, 2027. Work and school accounts move to Microsoft Planner. Personal users, including Microsoft 365 subscribers, get no Microsoft replacement and must export their tasks before the shutdown. (Neowin, Windows Latest)

AI Papers & Discussions

The Lean and Mathlib communities say they have "full confidence" that Levent Alpöge's Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct. The proof is a 17-million-line Lean library. Dozens of people compiled it independently, and experts checked that the proved statement matches Mathlib's definition. Lean's kernel and independent proof checkers accepted the exported proof, including two that carry a formal proof that they cannot accept a false one. This answers yesterday's open question of whether the proof checks. Mathematicians still have no human-readable proof to read. (Lean community, Scott Aaronson, HN)

Frontier models can often name the author of a single text (arXiv:2611.26216). With a plain zero-shot prompt and no tools, Claude Fable 5.2 named the author of 39% of public peer reviews from October-November 2026, and 78% of a subset from Nature. It identified the username behind 69% of comments from active Redditors. Opus 5.5 named the right lab for 98% of AI-written texts. For anyone with enough writing online, that is a larger threat to anonymity than commonly assumed.

Capping rollout length in RL training also caps how far a model scales at test time (arXiv:2611.26172). After RLVR training with a fixed budget, models barely use tokens beyond it. They beat the base model near that budget but fall below it when given more compute. The effect held for three 2-4B models and transferred from math to science and coding. SFT on artificially truncated traces reproduces it, so the cause is length-censored training, not RL itself.

IBM reports that the right amount of agent memory depends on the model (arXiv:2611.26361). The pipeline mines lessons from an agent's own runs and merges them into guidelines, and it ships in watsonx Orchestrate. DeepSeek-V3.2 gained 9.5 points on AppWorld with all 191 guidelines. gpt-oss-120b did better with a selected 29 than with all 199. The paper also lists failures, including a swallowed API error that failed every task while writing a plausible log.

Peer reviews of the same paper are converging as LLM use grows (arXiv:2611.26286). At ICLR, overlap between reviews of one paper rose from 12.0% in 2026 to 21.3% in 2027, with rises at NeurIPS, ICML and two journals. A third ICLR review added 5.46 new problems in 2027, down from 6.12. Reviews flagged as LLM-written overlap each other more than unflagged ones.
