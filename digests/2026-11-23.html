Financial News

CrowdStrike is in talks to buy Zscaler, Bloomberg reported. The deal would create a company worth about $265 billion and would be among the largest in cybersecurity. Zscaler rose about 16% after hours on Friday from a $32 billion market value. Neither company has confirmed the talks, and no price has been reported. (Bloomberg, Reuters via Investing.com)

Amazon is close to buying the AI startup Decart for $7.5 billion, the FT reported. Decart's software lets models run efficiently across Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs and Amazon's Trainium chips. The price is almost double the $4 billion valuation Decart reached in May, when Nvidia was among its investors. An announcement could come this week. (GuruFocus, Business Upturn)

Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are asking for up to 45% more for AI servers after contracts were signed, the FT reported. The suppliers blame memory, which JPMorgan estimates has risen more than 400% in price since early 2024. Trading firm XTX Markets has sued Dell in Ireland over a fixed-price $549 million order. Dell wants at least $62 million more for it. (MLQ, Aroged)

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants Nvidia to hire its next CEO from outside the company. He said the board needs a "dramatic shake-up" and is pressing Nvidia on whether its chips reached China in breach of export law. Elon Musk has called for Jensen Huang's return. The board is focused on its own investigation rather than reinstating him. (Yahoo Finance, World Today Journal)

October PCE inflation, revised third-quarter GDP and durable goods orders all land on Wednesday. Economists expect headline and core PCE to rise 0.3% each, after September's 0.5% headline rise. GDP growth is expected to be revised up to 3.5% from 3.4%. The Fed's Beige Book follows that afternoon. Markets close on Thursday for Thanksgiving and at 1pm ET on Friday. (FXEmpire, Bitcoin World)

HPE and Zoom report on Monday, Dell on Tuesday and Deere on Wednesday. Dell's results come days after the FT report on its server price rises. Monday also brings the flash PMIs, forecast at 54.5 for manufacturing and 55.6 for services, and a speech by New York Fed President John Williams. (FXEmpire)

Tech News

New US export rules have stopped some consumer-chip shipments to China. Since November 19, foundries must treat chips made at 16nm, 14nm or finer as controlled unless the designer is on a US whitelist. TSMC and Samsung have reportedly paused some shipments to Chinese designers. Chips for phones, TVs and cars are affected, not only AI accelerators. (Tom's Hardware, Nikkei Asia, TrendForce)

China's Ministry of State Security has run a campaign for at least two years to get advanced AI chips for Chinese companies, the New York Times reported. A working group reports directly to Minister Chen Yixin, officials outside China told the paper. It helps companies acquire and smuggle chips and gathers intelligence on the chip supply chain. (Daily Excelsior, MLQ)

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center. Police across the US use the database to share warrant and criminal records. It is the second reported FBI breach in two months. It is not yet known whether records were taken. (BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek)

OpenRouter dropped three inference providers after researchers found they broke their data policies. Dragos researchers found the problems. Together disclosed prompt leakage for requests in June and July. OpenRouter removed 125 endpoints from its zero-data-retention tier and says its own systems were not breached. (OpenRouter, HN discussion)

Tesla began rolling out Full Self-Driving v15 to early-access drivers. Its Reality View draws a generated overhead view of the car's surroundings instead of fixed 3D models. Tesla claims a 25x improvement in critical interventions but gives no baseline. Wider release is expected in December. (Not a Tesla App, Tesla North)

OpenAI is testing Dots, its always-on background agents, with some ChatGPT Plus users. Dots run between conversations and can be reached in ChatGPT, Slack or Teams. They were previously limited to Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise. All US Business Standard workspaces get them from November 19. (Engadget, tbreak)

Toyota, Nissan and Honda will standardize some parts to cut costs. They start with steel quality criteria, plastics and resin protocols, and one color code for wiring harnesses. The first standards take effect in 2027. The aim is to compete with cheaper Chinese carmakers. (Car and Driver, HN discussion)

AI Papers & Discussions

OpenAI proposes perplexity as the shared yardstick for AI progress, and as the trigger for development limits. Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki gives log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5, 0.91 for GPT-4 and under 0.80 for recent models. He expects transformative capability around 0.68 to 0.70. OpenAI wants labs to publish the figure and pause at agreed thresholds until alignment milestones are met. (OpenAI, HN discussion)

An estimate says 87% of Anthropic's power will come from gas and 11% from coal in 2028. The Better Data Center Project mapped 9.6 GW of Anthropic's contracted sites and modelled grid supply with the EPA's AVERT tool. Its mid case is 32 million tonnes of CO2e a year. That is about the 2025 Scope 2 emissions of Meta, Microsoft and Google combined. The HN thread drew 735 comments. (Better Data Center Project, HN discussion)

Exact arithmetic circuits can be compiled into a pretrained model's weights with no training (arXiv:2611.26450). The method changes under 0.06% of weights. Qwen 3.5 4B then gets 100% on 6-digit arithmetic, against 61.7% after LoRA fine-tuning on 12,800 examples. The model's ordinary language ability is preserved. It suggests exact skills can be installed by hand rather than learned.

Reflection's Beam technical report describes one of the largest open RL runs yet (arXiv:2611.26358). Post-training generated over 100 million rollouts on 10,500 GB300 GPUs over four weeks. Reflection reports that scores kept rising with RL compute and showed no plateau. Beam is a 501B mixture-of-experts model with 23B active parameters, released under Apache 2.0 in October.

Reasoning-vs-Knowledge Watch

GLiDE distills reasoning models into decision models that answer in one forward pass (arXiv:2611.26509). The 27B model scores 73.37 with 2.1% calibration error at a 92.9 ms median latency. It tests directly whether reasoning's gains survive without generating any reasoning text.

A geography study separates what a model memorized from what it can generalize (arXiv:2611.25703). It scores models on recalling place descriptions seen in training and on predicting descriptions for unseen regions of Los Angeles and London. This gives a clean benchmark split between stored knowledge and generalization.

OpenAI says it now blocks almost all attempts to extract reasoning traces for distillation. Attack success on earlier-request traces fell from 54.0% to 0.2% between GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.2 Astra. It names accounts tied to Moonshot AI and DeepSeek. Small reasoning models trained on frontier traces are losing their main source. (OpenAI)

Edit-based training supervises a reasoning model only where its trace first goes wrong (arXiv:2611.26342). An editor marks the first failure in a sampled trace and proposes a minimal fix, and the model trains only on that span. It offers finer credit assignment than whole-trace fine-tuning or trajectory-level RL.
