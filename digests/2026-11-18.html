Market Overview

US stocks fell for a second day on Tuesday. The S&P 500 lost 1.34% to 7,317, the Dow dropped 916 points (1.78%) and the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.32%, led by software stocks. Nvidia reports third-quarter results after the US close, about 08:20 Sydney time today, so the numbers were not out when this digest was written. The 10-year Treasury yield sat at 5.13%. Financial News

US retail sales rose 0.2% in October, beating the 0.1% forecast. Sales excluding autos rose 0.6% as car dealers fell 1.6% and online retailers rose 1.2%. (Bloomberg)

St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said the economy will likely need several rate increases before spring 2027. Traders price a 95% chance of a quarter-point hike in December, according to CME FedWatch. (CNBC)

Salesforce fell about 10% as software stocks sold off. Investors questioned how fast its Agentforce AI agents turn into revenue. Oracle, Palo Alto Networks and Microsoft each fell 3-4%. (GuruFocus, CNBC movers)

The White House is pressing ahead with a ban on new Chinese optical transceivers for data centers. Amazon, Google and Meta lobbied against it, saying compliance could take up to a decade. US transceiver makers Coherent and Lumentum rose 11% and 5.6%. (Reuters via Yahoo)

Home Depot named CFO Richard McPhail to replace CEO Ted Decker. Its third-quarter revenue rose 4.9% to $43.4 billion and beat estimates, but the stock fell about 2%. (Yahoo Finance)

Disney rose 2% after fourth-quarter earnings of $1.77 a share beat the $1.67 forecast. (CNBC)

Jensen Huang said he would be "honored" to return as Nvidia CEO if the board wanted him. It was his first statement since resigning last Thursday. He will also give transcribed interviews to three House committees on December 1. (CNBC, NPR)

Tech News

OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol. Astra targets coding and research, and its API price drops 20% from GPT-6 Astra. OpenAI says Sol comes within 0.5 points of Astra on FrontierCode 2.0 at about a fifth of the cost per task. (OpenAI, HN discussion)

Microsoft released MAI Code 2 and MAI Code 2 Flash in GitHub Copilot. Microsoft claims Code 2 reaches 96% of Claude Opus 5.5's score at 5% of its cost across four benchmarks. Nobody has verified that independently yet. (GitHub Changelog)

Attackers used a flaw in Optimizely's signup flow to plant malware on 178 organizations' websites. The script skimmed card details and swapped crypto wallet addresses for visitors to sites including CNN, BBC, Zoom and GitLab. Optimizely says the flaw is fixed. (BleepingComputer, Report URI)

Windows 11 records which coding agents you run and can send that to Microsoft. A researcher found one bit per tool, covering the claude, codex, copilot and agy CLIs plus Cursor and other desktop apps. It uploads only if optional diagnostic data is on, and no prompts or code were found in the report. (Xusheng Li, HN discussion)

iCloud, the App Store, Apple Pay and other Apple services went down on Tuesday. Apple blamed an internal update and ruled out an attack. Some business and education services stayed down after the rest recovered. (9to5Mac)

A highly classified US national security communications network went down. CNN and Reuters reported the outage on Tuesday. (Reuters)

Carnegie Mellon is putting computer science courses on YouTube for free. It was the top Hacker News story of the day with over 2,200 points. (CMU, HN discussion)

AI Papers & Discussions

Experienced agents fail where novices succeed after a tool changes silently (arXiv:2611.20922). Agents with memory copy their earlier tool call instead of reading the new interface, then report success. The authors call it Experience Rot. It appears in every memory form they tested and grows with experience. Announcing the update barely helps. This matters for any agent that reuses its own notes.

Some new base models can recite benchmark test items they were never shown (arXiv:2611.21315). Prompted with a few examples, ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base wrote out 96% of HumanEval problems with their reference solutions, against 9% for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base. The paper argues contamination has moved from post-training into pre-training and proposes an Anti-Hack score.

Reward hacking can be measured exactly when the true reward is computable (arXiv:2611.21538). The authors degrade a verifier seven ways and train against each while an exact oracle scores every rollout. An optimistic verifier is hacked by padding: its proxy score rises to 0.94 while true faithfulness falls. A cheap audit of the verifier before training predicts which failure a run will show.

Most models give confident wrong answers when their simulator is missing physics (arXiv:2611.21541). SciResearch-Eval has agents design heat experiments and report 95% intervals. With a flawed simulator, most models' intervals held the truth 20-62% of the time, against 94% for GPT-6 Astra and 99% for Claude Fable 5.2. It tests whether an agent notices its tools are wrong, which most benchmarks never check.
