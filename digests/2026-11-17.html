Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-11-17

Market Overview

US stocks fell hard on Monday as tech sold off before Nvidia's results. At 3pm New York time, with an hour of trading left, the S&P 500 was down 1.87% at 7,420.57. The Nasdaq was down 2.35% and the Dow 1.37%. US crude fell 3.3% to $85.62 as tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz recovered. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.12%, and bitcoin held near $65,000. The closing figures land after this digest was sent. (Yahoo Finance)

Financial News

Nvidia fell about 4% on reports that its board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back. The FT had reported that Huang discussed a comeback with allies. Nvidia reports after the close on Tuesday, which is 08:20 Wednesday in Sydney. Analysts expect about $109 billion in revenue and $2.47 a share in adjusted earnings. (Reuters, Saxo, HN)

Japan's economy shrank at a 0.1% annualised rate in the third quarter. Economists had expected growth of 0.2%. It is the first contraction in four quarters. Private consumption fell 0.3% from the previous quarter, its second straight drop, while nominal GDP grew 1.6% annualised. (Seoul Economic Daily, Xinhua)

The New York Fed's factory index fell to 7.8 in November from 25.5. Economists had expected 18.0. New orders dropped to 3.0 from 23.2. Hiring reached a four-year high, and fewer manufacturers reported rising input costs. (MarketScreener, MLQ)

Customers of Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and US Bank could not reach their accounts online on Monday. Chase told AP the cause was a technical bug, not a cyberattack. Zelle said its own network was working and that delays came from the banks. Bank stocks fell with the market. (AP, Forbes)

Nearly one in three Amazon Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals saved shoppers nothing, a watchdog found. Groundwork Collaborative tracked more than 350 products through the 2024 and 2025 sales. Nearly one in ten cost more during the sale than in the month before. For those, the median sale price was 19% above the recent low. (Groundwork, HN)

Tech News

Alibaba released Qwen4-Max. It is a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 71 billion active parameters and a 1M-token context. It beats Opus 5.5 on DeepSWE v1.1, 75.7 to 74.2, but trails it on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 48.5 to 59.6. Artificial Analysis ranks it ninth overall. It was the top Hacker News story on Monday. (Qwen, MarkTechPost, AgentsPulse, HN)

ULA lost a Vulcan rocket and 40 Amazon Leo satellites on Sunday. The Centaur upper stage failed about ten minutes into flight. It is ULA's first total mission failure in 20 years. The launch was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has bought for its Starlink rival. (Spaceflight Now, HN)

Microsoft traced last week's Microsoft 365 outage to a Windows bug it shipped in July. The bug caused lock contention on networking gateway servers. Within two hours more than 80% of them had stopped responding. Exchange Online was hit hardest, and the outage ran almost 24 hours from 16:20 UTC on 12 November. (Wccftech, BleepingComputer)

A now-fixed bug let any website run its own JavaScript on every site through uBlock Origin. A malicious page could drive the element picker into adding trusted filters, which execute code. The Firefox build was most exposed, and uBO Lite had a milder version of the flaw. (iter.ca, HN)

Attackers are re-registering expired Bitly links inside old posts. On 15 November the link in a 2011 Burger King tweet was re-pointed to Wikipedia's list of Nazi concentration camps. Bitly confirmed that a flaw let bad actors reuse old, inactive links. It says it is suspending the accounts and disabling the links. (Ransom News, HN)

Coles is investigating a cyber security incident. It is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and has found no sign that customer, staff or supplier data was taken. Stores, the app and online shopping are running normally. Coles has not said what kind of incident it is or which systems are involved. (Computer Weekly, Coles)

AI Papers & Discussions

The best model passes 38% of real spreadsheet tasks in GDP.xlsx (arXiv:2611.17766). Professionals in 13 domains wrote 70 requests over workbooks holding 2.18 million filled cells. A pass means meeting every rubric item. Gemini 4 Argon leads at 38.3%, and the median across 20 model setups is 19.3%. Only three tasks are public, so the leaderboard is hard to train against. (arXiv)

An activation edit can make a reasoning model believe it was asked something else, and it then defends that belief (arXiv:2611.17958). Across six models, the injected topic became the task the model reported in 47 of 48 cases. Told the true request in plain text, models invented reasons the user had changed their mind and answered the injection anyway. The chain of thought was corrupted along with the answer, which limits what monitoring it can catch. (arXiv)

A model that continues another model's conversation picks up its fingerprint (arXiv:2611.18104). An API relay could serve the first turns with the model you paid for and switch to a cheaper one. After such a switch, a classifier's detection of DeepSeek standing in for Claude Sonnet fell from 0.97 to 0.72. The authors' fix, Lineup, compares a reply with samples from the claimed model on the same history. It caught 0.81 of swapped sessions after four turns. (arXiv)

LLM reviewers take about half their criticisms from a paper's appendix (arXiv:2611.17811). The study covers 2,000 papers from ACL, EMNLP, COLM and ICLR, reviewed by recent GPT and Claude models. Human reviewers draw 8.4% of their weaknesses from the appendix. The models' appendix points are mostly fine technical details, not the paper's core idea. (arXiv)

Blog discussion: what "the proof is checked in Lean" does and does not tell you. The Xena Project post lists four checks:

  • the Lean statement matches the informal theorem;
  • the code compiles;
  • the proof does not exploit redefinitions;
  • no kernel bug is involved.

In one recent OpenAI batch of formal proofs, the post counts about 38 whose Lean code does not even state the paper's main theorem. (Xena Project, HN)
