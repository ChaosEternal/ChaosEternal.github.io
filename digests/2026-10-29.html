Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-10-29

Market Overview

US stocks edged up on Wednesday after the Fed held rates. The Dow rose 0.19% to 52,475.10, the S&P 500 0.28% to 7,743.98 and the Nasdaq 0.27% to 26,549.85. Chip stocks gained for a third straight day. December crude rose 2.6% to $102.47, and the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.05%. Bitcoin was up about 0.9% at $81,430. Alphabet fell after hours on its results. Microsoft also reported after the close, but this digest could not confirm its figures when it was written. (Yahoo Finance)

Financial News

The Fed held rates at 3.75% to 4%, and two officials voted for a hike. The vote was 10-2, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting. The statement upgraded growth to "expanding at a strong pace" and said inflation "remains elevated". The Fed raised rates by a quarter point in September, its first increase since July 2023. (CNBC, Fox Business)

Alphabet shares fell about 7% after hours as revenue missed while Google Cloud beat. Revenue was $127.58 billion against $127.93 billion expected. Google Services brought in $96.30 billion against $98.4 billion expected. Google Cloud reached $30.71 billion against $29.1 billion expected. Capex was $51.36 billion, below the $55.78 billion forecast. (24/7 Wall St., CNBC)

SK Hynix posted record quarterly revenue above 100 trillion won and said the memory shortage will last until 2030. Revenue was 104.62 trillion won, up 32% on the quarter. Operating profit was 83.33 trillion won, a margin of about 80%. The company plans about 100 trillion won in shareholder returns for 2026, including a 20 trillion won buyback. Its US-listed shares rose about 8%. (Quartz, TNW)

Oracle jumped 12% after targeting at least $225 billion in revenue for fiscal 2030. That implies more than 35% average annual growth. Oracle also capped new fundraising through fiscal 2030 at $20 billion, half equity and half debt. It expects growth to fund itself from fiscal 2029. (CNBC, Oracle)

Vertiv rose 17.3% after organic orders more than doubled. Third-quarter sales grew 61% to $4.31 billion, and adjusted EPS of $2.21 beat the $1.82 consensus. Vertiv raised its full-year sales forecast by $650 million. (Vertiv release, StockTitan)

Enphase, F5 and Logitech fell hard on guidance. The losses were:

  • F5 fell 19.8% after forecasting 8% to 9% revenue growth for fiscal 2027, down from 11%.
  • Enphase fell 17.1% on soft residential solar demand and the expiry of the Section 25D tax credit.
  • Logitech fell 12.1% on a $70 million to $80 million hit from a supplier shutdown.

(StockTitan)

Wendy's jumped 15% on a $1.4 billion take-private offer from Nelson Peltz's Trian. The group offers $7.35 a share in cash, about a 20% premium to Tuesday's close. Trian already owns about 16.3% of the stock. Wendy's has reported six straight quarters of falling same-store sales. (CNBC, Washington Post)

Genmab rose 29.9% after a Phase 3 head-and-neck cancer trial cut the risk of death by 50%. The experimental drug was given in combination with another cancer treatment. (StockTitan)

Tech News

Google released Gemini 4 Carbon, but only to a subset of cyber defenders for now. Google calls it its strongest model, built for cybersecurity and long agentic tasks. It scores 59 on Artificial Analysis's new Intelligence Index v5, one point above Claude Opus 5.5. It reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. (Google, HN)

Artificial Analysis rebuilt its Intelligence Index, and 60% of it now uses private or held-out test sets. Version 5 adds FrontierCode 2.0, which grades whether a maintainer would merge an agent's change. It also adds Terminal-Bench-Science. GDPval-AA, SciCode and AA-LCR were dropped. Before Carbon, the top three were Claude Opus 5.5 at 58, GPT-6 Astra at 57 and GPT-6.1 Sol at 55. (Artificial Analysis, HN)

Apple announced a touchscreen MacBook Pro with a tandem OLED display. Apple calls the screen PROLED XDR, and it brings the Dynamic Island to the Mac. The 14-inch and 16-inch models use M5 Pro or M5 Max chips. Prices start at $2,899 for the 14-inch and $3,399 for the 16-inch. Pre-orders opened Wednesday, and machines ship November 6. (Apple, Tom's Guide)

A 16-year-old flaw in UEFI reference firmware lets attackers run code before Secure Boot. CVE-2026-97631 is a memory-corruption bug in the ICMP code of EDK II, rated 9.5 out of 10. It is reachable only while a machine boots over the network. EDK II underlies firmware from AMI, Insyde and Phoenix. AMI published the details ahead of a coordinated disclosure planned for January, and the patch was merged October 15. (The Hacker News, HN)

Apple's git on macOS still trusts the compromised eMudhra certificate authorities. The researchers say Apple was told on October 17. macOS 27.2, released October 26, did not fix it and also undid the manual workaround. Apple has published a support note on the compromised intermediates. (researchers, Apple, HN)

The FCC approved Amazon's $11.5 billion purchase of Globalstar for its Leo satellite network. The deal adds about two dozen satellites and spectrum that lets phones connect directly to satellites. Globalstar runs Emergency SOS on iPhone, and Amazon has agreed with Apple to keep it running. Amazon has launched nearly 400 Leo satellites so far. (DCD, HN)

Anthropic reported early drug-development results with six partners and bought a biotech. Generate Biomedicines designed a CAR-T therapy 70% shorter than the standard one that performed 10 times better in tests. Enveda cut manufacturing document review time by 89%. Anthropic acquired Mithril Medicine and aims to take a muscular dystrophy candidate into human trials by late 2028. (Anthropic, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

A typed protocol stopped an agent from changing bookings without the customer's "yes" (arXiv:2610.37461). Concordat treats an agent as a finite-state protocol and offers the model only the tools legal in its current state. On tau-bench airline tasks with customers pressuring it to skip confirmation, the standard agent made unconfirmed changes in 13 of 50 tasks with gpt-4.1. With Claude Haiku 5.5 it did so in 29 of 128 runs. The Concordat agent made none, with no significant loss in task success. tau-bench's own reward missed most of the violations.

A trained agent's skill is partly its harness, and changing the harness can break it (arXiv:2610.37255). The authors split deployed return into the best return a harness offers, minus what the policy loses to its own mistakes. On MiniWoB++, reversing the order of the observation fields changes no optimal decision. It still drops a trained model's success from 85.0% to 19.2%. A harness that a fixed-model comparison rejects can still win once the model is trained under it.

A popular confidence score for pruning reasoning chains mostly measures chain length (arXiv:2610.36709). The method keeps chains whose lowest sliding-window token confidence is high. Over 16 chains for each of 500 math problems, ranking by length alone did as well or better. Once length is accounted for, the score says nothing about correctness. For early stopping, simply sampling fewer chains was just as good.

Compressing a group discussion into memory fails in predictable ways (arXiv:2610.37624). Keeping the claims most relevant to the decision readmits decoys, which are also on topic. Keeping the rarest claims is no better than random on average. The decision model also over-weights facts attributed to many agents, and removing the attribution tags raised accuracy by 13 to 24 points. In natural discussions the full transcript scored lower than no notes at all.
