Market Overview

US stocks fell on Thursday as Alphabet dragged tech lower. The S&P 500 lost 0.30% to 7,720.39, the Dow 0.25% to 52,342.56 and the Nasdaq 1.38% to 26,184.73. Small caps went the other way, with the Russell 2000 up 0.94%. Alphabet fell about 12% in the regular session, extending the after-hours drop reported yesterday. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.99%. Crude held near $102.84, gold slipped 0.4% to $4,035 and bitcoin fell 1.2% to about $80,285. (Yahoo Finance)

Financial News

US growth beat forecasts in the third quarter, and inflation edged up. Real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate, up from 2.2% in the second quarter and above forecasts of 3.1% to 3.2%. September's PCE price index rose 3.6% from a year earlier, and core PCE 3.1%. Both were in line with forecasts and slightly above August. (BEA, Invezz)

The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.5%, and Christine Lagarde said she will leave early. She steps down on 7 February 2027, about nine months before her term ends. The ECB gave no reason. Euro-area inflation is 3.8%, and the bank blamed energy prices. Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos and the Netherlands' Klaas Knot are named as leading successors. (Euronews, ECB)

ServiceNow had its worst day since its 2012 listing, falling about 24%. Third-quarter revenue of $4.11 billion beat estimates. The trigger was its fourth-quarter outlook for contracted revenue growth: 18.5%, against about 19% expected and 21% last quarter. Salesforce fell 6% and Workday 5% in sympathy. (Benzinga, 24/7 Wall St.)

Intel beat on the quarter but fell about 20% after hours on its outlook. Revenue rose 21% to $16.5 billion, and adjusted EPS of $0.52 beat the $0.40 expected. Intel guided fourth-quarter adjusted EPS to $0.25 and gross margin to 38%, down from 44.1%. (MarketBeat, 24/7 Wall St.)

Amazon narrowly missed estimates, and its shares fell about 8% after hours. Revenue was $201.94 billion against $202.26 billion expected. EPS was $1.89 against $1.96. (CNBC, MarketBeat)

Microsoft beat on both lines, and its shares barely moved. Revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion against $90.6 billion expected. Net income rose 32% to $36.6 billion, and EPS of $4.89 beat the $4.71 consensus. (Microsoft release, MarketBeat)

Tech News

DoorDash agreed to buy Instacart for about $17 billion. It is paying $65 a share, a 26% premium to Instacart's 22 October close. At least 75% of the price is cash. The deal needs US, Canadian and Israeli competition approval, and DoorDash expects it to close between late 2027 and mid-2028. (DoorDash, HN)

Mistral released the weights of Mistral Large 4, a one-trillion-parameter model. It is a multimodal mixture-of-experts model that handles both instruction and reasoning modes. The licence is a modified MIT that requires a separate deal only for commercial hosted services. Mistral says it leads open-weight models on FrontierCode 2.0 and Terminal-Bench 4.0. It also says human raters placed it second only to Claude Opus 5.5 on code quality. (Mistral, HN)

Hacktivists published the names and contact details of more than 40,000 Homeland Security employees. That is about a fifth of the department's workforce. The list includes over 21,000 Customs and Border Protection staff and over 10,000 from ICE and Homeland Security Investigations. The group says it got in by subverting DHS's single sign-on system. DHS says it is "aware of a potential breach". (NPR, HN)

A developer measured Claude Code spending 16.4% of its budget on status labels for background agents. About every 30 seconds, it sends each background agent's whole transcript to that agent's model, usually Opus, to get a 3-to-5-word label for the agents panel. The author measured this with OpenTelemetry on versions 2.1.257 to 2.1.312. The author cites others who measured 18% to 26% overhead. (gist, HN)

A single malformed SMS could crash the phone process on Android 14 to 17. A WAP Push message carrying an oversized length field makes com.android.phone allocate a negative-sized array and die. Pixel and Samsung devices were affected. Google fixed it in the October 2026 security update, five months after the report. (write-up, HN)

Stack Overflow now gets fewer than 1,000 questions a month, according to a public Data Explorer query. The post drew 657 comments, the most of any HN story today. (query, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

Automatic skill optimizers mostly add cost, not success (arXiv:2610.37201). The authors tested three optimizers that rewrite an agent's skill text and keep edits that score better on a small dev set. Across three benchmarks and two models, the learned skills beat their hand-written seeds by 0.1 points on average. Their dev-set gains vanished on test. Picking the best of several candidates on 30 tasks produced gains that large even when no candidate had changed. The learned skills also cost about 16% more input tokens, mostly from added "re-check your work" rules.

In a clinical coding benchmark, a complete specification did the work credited to skills (arXiv:2610.37459). M4Bench has 28 tasks deriving clinical variables from hospital records. Skills raised reward by 0.136 for Codex agents. On a control subset, a written specification alone raised it by 0.162, and skills added only 0.024 on top. Decoy skills scored the same as no skill. Only 25 of 280 with-skill runs passed every strict check.

Learned attention gates cut a strong prompt-injection attack from 97% success to 10% (arXiv:2610.37657). Heads of Security trains a gate on a small set of attention heads to separate trusted from untrusted tokens. Against the RL-Hammer attack on GPT-OSS-20B, success fell from 97% to 10%. The fine-tuning defense SecAlign allowed 85%. The gates kept the base model's scores on standard benchmarks, where the two baseline defenses lost ground.

Only one of 12 models discovered a changed law of physics without being told to look (arXiv:2610.37649). LongChang builds simulated worlds where a law differs from ordinary physics, such as heavier objects falling more slowly. Models explore freely with no research question. GPT-6-Astra found the changed law 16.7% of the time, and the other 11 models never did.

Four of five LLM agents ran a vending business worse than a simple rule-based controller (arXiv:2610.36980). AutoVend has one agent manage 20 machines for 180 simulated days, with procurement delays, expiring stock and running costs. Ranking models by return per dollar of inference changed the order from ranking by return alone.
