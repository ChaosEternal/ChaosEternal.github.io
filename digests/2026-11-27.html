Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-11-27

Market Overview

US markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. They reopen on Friday for a shortened session that ends at 1 p.m. Eastern. Elsewhere, buyers returned after Wednesday's AI sell-off. Germany's DAX rose 1.9%, France's CAC 40 rose 1.8% and the FTSE 100 rose 1.6%. Taiwan's Taiex gained 2.5% and the Nikkei 0.9%. S&P 500 futures were up about 0.5%. (AP via BNN Bloomberg)

Financial News

S&P put Nvidia's AA credit rating on watch for a downgrade, citing governance. It could cut the rating one notch to AA- within 90 days. S&P lowered its management and governance score to neutral and named key-person risk and missing succession planning after Jensen Huang's exit and return. It will meet the board before deciding. (The Next Web, Proactive)

Nvidia stopped AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. Dell and Supermicro told customers there that Nvidia will not authorise their orders. New US rules that took effect last week require licences for those countries, which Washington sees as routes for chips to reach China. Nvidia booked $104 billion of 2025 revenue through its Singapore subsidiary. (The National, Reuters via US News)

SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts in an AI restructuring. That is at least 9% of its workforce. Every department has been asked to find cuts, with product, engineering and sales in focus. It would be SAP's second restructuring of this size in under three years, and the company has not confirmed it. (Bloomberg, MarketScreener)

Memory and AI investment names led Asia's rebound. Kioxia jumped 12.6%, SoftBank Group 7.3% and SK Hynix 2.2%. TSMC slipped 0.2%. (AP via BNN Bloomberg)

US online holiday spending reached $82.5 billion from November 1 to 22, up 9.1% on last year. Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion of online sales on Black Friday itself, up 9.2%. Bain expects Black Friday through Cyber Monday retail sales to top $85 billion. (Digital Commerce 360, Bain)

Gold and bitcoin both fell about 3.6% after the hot PCE report. Gold traded near $3,535 an ounce and bitcoin near $59,300. Oil went the other way on Middle East tensions, with Brent up 0.5% to $91.09. (FXStreet, BNN Bloomberg)

Tech News

The New York Times told its freelance photographers not to use Photoshop 2027 on assignment. Its email says the release uses generative AI "in ways that may not be apparent to users" and can write metadata labelling a photo as AI-generated. The update adds an AI Assisted Editor, prompt-based editing and AI recomposition. The Hacker News thread drew 619 points. (PetaPixel, HN)

OpenAI fixed a GPT-6.2 billing error, so paid users now hit their limits sooner. Astra allowances run out 40% faster and Sol 25% faster. The launch had wrongly discounted usage since November 17. Paying subscribers get one banked allowance reset in compensation. (TechRadar, HN)

The FCC gave SpaceX conditional approval for up to 1 million orbital data-center satellites. The constellation is called Starmind and would link to Starlink by laser. Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society objected. Debris plans above 600 km are still unapproved, and SpaceX expects to start deploying in 2028. (TechCrunch, CNBC)

Anthropic moved its Messages API from Python to Rust, with Claude writing much of the code. CPU per request fell about 70%, memory per request over 80%, and median time to first token about 20%. Anthropic did not trust the tests, so it checked the new version against the old one on live production traffic. (Anthropic, TechRepublic)

Todoist says a hacker stole data on about 6% of its customers. The attacker used stolen credentials on November 22 and 23 to reach source control and then production. Emails, account creation dates and older password hashes were taken. For a tiny share of users the attacker also searched tasks for API keys. (TechCrunch)

Nfs2root is a 12-year-old pre-authentication remote root bug in the Linux kernel's NFS server. It is CVE-2026-99362, a heap overflow reached through NFSv4 session slots. Fixed kernels include 6.12.117, 6.18.61 and 7.3.2. Separately, Hanno Böck noted on oss-security that Ubuntu still had no fix for the NoisyTCP kernel bug nine days after disclosure. (opensec, oss-security, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

Anthropic staff and Claude cut the Lean proof of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions from about 17 million lines to 76,000. It is built on Levent Alpöge's proof with Claude. A readable 300,000-line edition separates known mathematics from new mathematics and is documented throughout. It has passed machine checks only, and no mathematician has read it through. Commenters asked whether this answers Tao's worry about huge proofs that carry no insight. (repo, HN)

Two researchers say Claude Fable 5.2 proved NP_R ≠ PAR_R in the real-number model of computation. The proofs build on the recent VP ≠ VNP result and show the real permanent has no short real certificates. They are formalized in Lean, with cited results taken as hypotheses. Skeptics on Hacker News note that, unlike the Riemann statement, these definitions are not in Mathlib and nobody has checked them. (repo, HN)

Letting the model manage its own context beats harness compaction at tight budgets (arXiv:2611.34147). NVIDIA gave models a context API in its open-source agent harness. GPT 6.1 went from 21% to 56% on OfficeQA Pro v2 at a 32K budget, and Kimi needed up to 41% fewer input tokens. Reusing the KV cache of the unchanged suffix cut prefill compute by up to 15%. That matters for any long-running agent that compacts.

An audit finds no reliable evidence that agent skills hurt any SkillsBench task (arXiv:2611.33943). The authors reanalysed 7,554 trials at the source-code level. They found grader dependence, placeholder skill folders and a harness that silently added 48 built-in skills. No per-task drop survived multiple-testing correction, and 58% of cells could not have shown a drop at all. Work that prunes skills by per-task loss rests on noise.

The choice of attack policy can make an AI monitor look far stronger than it is (arXiv:2611.34934). On an ex-production OpenAI monitor, the measured false-negative rate ranged from 0.5% to 20.8% across optimisation levels of one method. Across methods it ranged from 6.4% to 23.0%. An evaluation using only basic attacks gives false confidence.
