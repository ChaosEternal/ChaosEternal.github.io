Market Overview

US stocks recovered more than half of last week's plunge on Monday after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. The S&P 500 rose nearly 5% and broke a seven-day losing streak. The Nasdaq Composite rallied 6.7%, and the Dow gained more than 1,500 points. The 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.93%. Thanksgiving week continues with Dell's results on Tuesday and the October PCE inflation report on Wednesday. (Yahoo Finance, AP via BNN Bloomberg)

Financial News

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO, and the stock jumped about 32%. It was Nvidia's best day since 2003 and added about $888 billion in market value, the largest one-day gain on record. The board said it received input from senior levels of the federal government and sees no national security risk. President Trump said he convinced the board. (CNBC, ABC News, Benzinga)

AI infrastructure stocks led the rebound. CoreWeave rose about 23% and Oracle about 12.5% by late morning. AMD gained about 9% and Micron about 6%. (AP via BNN Bloomberg, Fox Business)

AMD and Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN agreed an AI infrastructure deal worth up to $10 billion. It will deploy 500 megawatts of AMD-based compute across Saudi Arabia and the US over five years. HUMAIN runs the data centers and power, and AMD supplies chips and its ROCm software. (DCD)

Applied Digital signed an 18-year, $5.4 billion data-center lease with an unnamed hyperscaler. The lease covers 150 megawatts, with delivery starting in the first half of 2028. It lifts the company's contracted lease revenue above $41 billion. (Applied Digital)

HPE rose about 14% on Monday ahead of its results after the close. It had guided to fourth-quarter revenue of $13.9 billion to $14.8 billion. Consensus implied about 49% growth, which would be a record pace. (TradingKey, Tickeron)

Taiwan's stock market fell about 17% last week, its worst week since the early 1990s. On Monday South Korea's KOSPI fell almost 6% at its low before closing down about 3.2%. Shanghai fell about 1% and the Hang Seng about 0.7%. (investingLive)

Oil fell for a third straight week while gold rebounded above $3,600. Brent fell about 2% to near $90.60 a barrel and WTI fell about 1.5% to near $82. Spot gold rose as much as 2.3%. Bitcoin traded near $61,500. (HDFC Sky, Mitrade, KuCoin)

Tech News

Z.ai released GLM-5.4, its first loop transformer. The 750-billion-parameter coding model runs 28 layers of the GLM-5 base twice. Z.ai says it matches models of two to three trillion parameters. It adds vision for visual coding and is live for GLM Coding Plan users, with API access still pending. (Z.ai, MarkTechPost)

The Ladybird browser is now in alpha. It runs a new engine built from scratch rather than a fork, on macOS and Linux. The team warns that some sites will not work and the browser will sometimes crash. (Ladybird)

Kagi opened a public beta of Kagi Mail. It offers @kagimail.com addresses or custom domains, with a calendar, tracking protection and AI inbox sorting. It costs $99 a year after a 60-day trial and is included in Kagi Ultimate. (Kagi)

Deutsche Telekom is investing €100 million in Mistral AI. Telekom's Munich AI compute hub also signed its first industrial tenant. (VoIP Review)

A popular unofficial AArch64 reference site will close on 4 December at Arm's request. Jon's Arm Reference documents AArch64 instructions and system registers. The notice on the site gives Arm's request as the reason. (Jon's Arm Reference)

Apple removed the open-source FSNotes app from every EU App Store after nine years. The developer says Apple gave no clear reason and has not answered support requests since 5 November. The developer says the required EU Digital Services Act compliance information was already filed. (FSNotes on GitHub)

AI Papers & Discussions

Yann LeCun accused Dario Amodei of hyping AI dangers so that open models are outlawed. Amodei had told Congress that widely released open-weight models could be misused. Anthropic's published July position says it "has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models". The Hacker News thread drew 136 comments within hours. (LeCun on X, FourWeekMBA, HN)

Models memorize the hallucinations they produce during RL post-training (arXiv:2611.26863). Across six open models, recall of rewarded self-hallucinations rose 4.83%, against 0.17% for unrewarded ones. Models even memorize false facts they retract mid-reasoning. Reasoning-style RL recipes can therefore damage factual knowledge.

Benchmarks' Last Exam audits whether benchmark prompts, environments and graders agree (arXiv:2611.27622). It reviewed 749 tasks across eight frontier benchmarks. Revised closed-form benchmarks reach 92.5% verified validity, against 29.3% for multi-step agentic suites. 71.7% of confirmed defects withhold credit from correct answers, so leaderboards understate capability more often than they inflate it.

MemOnce cuts a 4B model's memory cache 1,313-fold at 1M tokens of context (arXiv:2611.27973). It stores a few final-layer hidden states and decodes them back into KV caches on demand, with sparse retrieval computed once across layers. Adapting Qwen3.5-4B also cut prefill latency 6.9-fold. That makes persistent long-horizon memory far cheaper to hold on a GPU.

A computable guardrail can be exact only if its policy is decidable (arXiv:2611.27260). For an undecidable policy, every guard misclassifies infinitely many strings. The authors apply this to arithmetic truth, program behaviour and harmful instructions. It sharpens the gatekeeping-impossibility line of work from 2611.13367.
