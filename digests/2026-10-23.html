Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-10-23

Market Overview

Tech sold off on Thursday as worries about AI spending returned. The S&P 500 fell about 1.2% and the Nasdaq more than 2%, its worst day since June. The Dow ended near flat. Sources disagree on the exact closing levels, so this digest does not quote them. Meta and Tesla led the decline, and AMD fell about 8%. Brent rose to about $113, its highest since May. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.95%. Bitcoin dropped 3.9% to about $81,300, and gold fell 2.6% to about $4,043. (Yahoo Finance, CNBC)

Financial News

Meta fell about 7% a day after its results, reversing its after-hours gain. The company announced a 2-for-1 split that gives each Class A holder one non-voting Class C share. The new shares start trading in late January 2027. The structure lets Meta raise money for its AI build-out while Mark Zuckerberg keeps control. (Fast Company, FXStreet)

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay the state $25 billion. A jury had found Meta misled users about data privacy, including over Cambridge Analytica, and counted more than 43 million violations of state consumer law. The judge said the law allowed a penalty above $219 billion. Meta says it will appeal. (NBC News, The Hill)

Tesla beat profit forecasts, but operating income fell 56% to $718 million. Revenue was flat at $28.05 billion. Adjusted earnings of $0.48 a share beat the $0.45 forecast. Automotive gross margin excluding credits rose to 18.1%, and free cash flow was $1.53 billion. Tesla said it now expects about $20 billion of capital spending this year. The stock fell about 3% on Thursday. (Electrek, Yahoo Finance)

Moderna fell about 24% after its melanoma vaccine data leaked early. The results surfaced on a medical news site around 12:30 p.m. ET, two days before their planned debut at the ESMO congress. The full data looked positive. The vaccine with Merck's Keytruda cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% compared with Keytruda alone. The stock had risen more than 530% this year. Merck fell 3.6%. (CNBC)

Micron workers in Taoyuan began Taiwan's first chip-industry strike. The 24-hour stoppage started at 9 a.m. on October 22, after 99% of voting members backed it. The union wants a one-off bonus of 83 months' salary and a permanent 15% share of quarterly operating profit. Micron settled with its Taichung union a day earlier. Taiwan makes 50% to 60% of Micron's output. (Focus Taiwan, Caixin)

US jobless claims fell to 194,000, a three-month low. Economists had expected 200,000. The four-week average fell to 197,750. (Bloomberg)

Tech News

Anthropic opened a waitlist for Claude Duet, a proactive agent running on Opus 5.5. Duet surfaces work that needs attention, runs tasks in the background, and asks before acting for the user. It is a research preview for US Pro and Max subscribers, at no extra cost. It runs as a separate app on iOS, Android and the web. (Anthropic, TestingCatalog)

Railway says an attacker may have read decrypted customer environment variables. Those often hold API keys and database credentials. Railway paused all deployments and told users to rotate every variable. It believes the attack is part of a series aimed at platform companies to reach AI inference providers. (Railway, HN, 571 points)

Amazon saw the PHP2Shell flaws exploited four days before they were disclosed. Its honeypots logged the first attempt on October 17, and disclosure came on October 21. Amazon says the bugs are now broadly exploited. Separately, a developer posted on HN that they had broken the embargo by accident. (AWS Security, HN)

eMudhra is leaving the global SSL/TLS certificate business. The move follows the compromise of eight of its intermediate certificate authorities. TLS was about 1% of its revenue. It will replace affected certificates free of charge and keep revocation running until every certificate expires or is replaced. mitmproxy has already added an --emudhra option. (eMudhra, HN, 786 points)

Quad9 will stop serving users in France on November 24. A French court ordered the free DNS resolver to block domains that beIN says carry pirated sports streams. Quad9 says it cannot comply without applying French law worldwide or geo-blocking. (Quad9, HN, 631 points)

Walmart opened an MCP server so AI agents can shop for customers. Agent Gateway lets approved agents search inventory and place orders without seeing payment details. Agent Profiles, due within a month, let customers set a spending cap per agent or require approval for each order. Amazon still blocks agents from buying on its site. (Walmart, PPC Land)

The Arc browser shuts down on December 22. The Browser Company says keeping a browser secure takes more work in the age of AI, so it is focusing on Dia. New Arc accounts stopped on October 22, and Dia imports Arc data. (Arc, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

A coding agent searching raw chat history beat Mem0 and Zep on long-conversation recall (arXiv:2610.29823). "MemoryTax" ran full context, BM25, Mem0, Zep and three coding agents on one LoCoMo conversation, with GPT-4o-mini, GPT-6 Luna and Claude Opus 5.5. Mem0 beat full context only with the weakest model, and building its memory cost more than a whole full-context run. A stronger reader could not recover what a weaker memory writer left out. With Opus 5.5, the Pi agent scored 99% at under half the cost of full context. It is one conversation and one run per cell, so treat it as a pointer rather than a result. It bears directly on memory-solution's design. (arXiv)

Document converters drop colour and highlighting, and agents become confidently wrong (arXiv:2610.30026). The task was to recover the requirements an author flagged in 76 real policy documents. Formatting-blind ingestion fell from F1 0.690 to 0.150 with docling and 0.075 with plain text. The gap held across five Claude models and GPT-5.6. Highlight and colour carried almost all the loss, while bold and italic barely mattered. About a third of 6,460 documents use colour or highlight on purpose. (arXiv)

Chain-of-thought monitorability can be trained back in (arXiv:2610.29734). The method maximises mutual information between the reasoning and the output, with no labels. It recovered most or all of the lost monitorability in three models whose reasoning had been degraded. On normal models it improved how well monitors and probes predicted unsafe behaviour. Yesterday's digest covered how reward misspecification erodes monitorability; this is a proposed repair. (arXiv)

Coding agents back up state before risky changes only 15% of the time (arXiv:2610.28934). RegretBench has 205 tasks built from cases where users regretted losing state. Six frontier models finished 86% of tasks but backed up first in only 15.2%. A plug-in layer that checks every tool call covered every risky action for $0.014 a task. (arXiv)

Models fail NP-complete problems far more often than their easy twins in the same words (arXiv:2610.30103). P2NPCBench pairs each NP-complete problem with a polynomial-time twin, such as 3-colouring against 2-colouring. Statements were rewritten until textbook cues were gone. Twelve models scored 35.4% on the hard side and 63.8% on the easy side. Removing the cues narrowed the gap from 24.8 to 14.6 points, so part of the gap was recall of familiar phrasing. (arXiv)
