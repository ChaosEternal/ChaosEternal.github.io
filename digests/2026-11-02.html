Tech News

A remotely triggerable Linux TCP bug is rated CVSS 9.8, and major distributions have not shipped fixes. CVE-2026-98432 is a use-after-free in the IPv4 TCP handshake. It fires before any application code runs, so any host accepting inbound TCP connections is exposed. Anthropic built a proof of concept that gets root without credentials on servers with multipath TCP listeners. Upstream fixes landed between 11 and 18 October. As of 31 October, Ubuntu still listed every release as vulnerable and Red Hat had no fix for RHEL 8, 9 or 10. Debian, SUSE, Amazon Linux and Oracle have shipped fixed kernels. (Atlasum, HN)

Anthropic changed Claude Code's prompt to steer it away from cloning existing games. Addy Osmani said the team added two sentences to the Opus 5.5 prompt, because using Claude to infringe IP breaks its usage policy. The new rule tells Claude to build original games, genre games and games from the user's own designs. Developers report that legitimate reverse-engineering work, such as the Reverse Engineer Anything tool, is now refused too. (PC Gamer, TechPowerUp, HN)

ChatGPT and Codex will share one usage allowance from December. It covers Plus, Pro and Business plans. The pool is measured in tokens rather than messages, so bigger models and longer tasks drain it faster. Instant chat will not count against it. OpenAI has not said how the weekly Pro caps interact with the shared pool. (Startup Fortune, HN)

A Minnesota police chief unplugged Flock's licence-plate cameras after the company ignored the city's cancellation. Plymouth's council voted unanimously in September to end the contract, and the city sent a formal cancellation letter. Flock did not reply, so Chief Eric Fadden disconnected the power himself. Flock still owns the hardware. The nearby city of Dayton says Flock installed and switched on two cameras against its instructions. (Tom's Hardware via Yahoo, The Drive)

A Brazilian labour court fined two law firms for hiding a prompt injection in a filing. White-on-white text told any AI reading the petition to respond superficially and not challenge the documents. The judiciary's Galileu system detected it and blocked processing. The 3rd Labour Court of Parauapebas fined the firms 10% of an R$842,000 claim, about US$16,000. (Jetstream, HN)

Andrej Karpathy built HackerTube, a video site curated only from Hacker News recommendations. Claude mined 40,000 HN comments from 2007 to 2026 that recommend a YouTube video. The result is 6,454 videos across 150 topics, with recommendations that need no account. It was the top Hacker News story of the last 36 hours, at almost 2,000 points. (Karpathy, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

arXiv posts no new listings at weekends, so the papers below come from Friday's batch and were not in the last three digests.

A linear probe can warn that an agent is about to leak a secret before it writes anything (arXiv:2610.40588). In six scenarios, a peer agent pressed a target for up to 20 rounds to reveal a secret such as a deploy token. On held-out Qwen3-32B episodes, flagging 5% of benign calls, the probe caught 82% of leaking calls. A zero-shot monitor model caught 26% at a similar rate. The authors are candid about limits: a bag-of-words classifier on the same labels came close, and the probe also flags permitted disclosures. Significance: the gain comes mostly from labelled training data, not from reading activations. (arXiv)

The Ramanujan Challenge drew an AI-assisted proof of one of its published open conjectures (arXiv:2610.40551). The challenge set research-level number-theory problems whose formulas can be checked numerically but whose proofs are hard. Some correct submissions differed from the official solutions. Judging the proofs at scale was the second problem. The authors' pipeline cut false-positive verdicts on natural-language proofs from 23% to 6%, compared with a one-shot LLM judge. Significance: proof grading, not proof generation, is becoming the bottleneck for research-level math benchmarks. (arXiv)

Which part of a transformer to loop depends on which resource is scarce (arXiv:2610.40325). The study trained dense models up to 1B parameters on up to 155B tokens with up to 32 loops. Looping the whole model gave the best quality for a fixed parameter count. Looping a middle block paid off better when training compute was the limit. Gains eventually saturate or reverse, and small per-loop LoRA adapters help. Significance: it is a practical recipe for depth without parameters, including a fix for the quality loss in parallel looped decoding. (arXiv)

A team of five Qwen3.8-27B agents scored 9.8 of 10 on LiveCodeBench Hard problems, against 8.0 for one Fable 5 call. The Memdoor workflow uses a planner, two ideators, a solver and a manager, each in fresh context and talking only through files. The author's own caveats matter: only the first ten problems were run, five times each, and contamination cannot be ruled out. On the full 100-problem set, the reference harness scored 92.4 and Fable 90.4. Significance: a small open model in a structured team roughly matches a frontier single call, on a sample too small to say more. (GitHub, HN)

GPT-5.6 Sol finished a peaceful-mode Factorio run, launching the rocket in 13.5 hours of game time. It ran in Codex on a low thinking level, driving the game through a small Python library. The author notes late-game scaling problems that Sol papered over with manual building. The gain over GPT-5.5 came mainly from Sol actually using transport belts. (Ryan Madden, HN)

Reasoning-vs-Knowledge Watch

Three papers this week, all in Friday's arXiv batch and none in the last three digests.

Synthetic "pre-pretraining" never beat plain natural-language training at matched compute (arXiv:2610.40051). The authors tried four synthetic curricula: k-Dyck languages, neural cellular automata, declarative reference corpora and code. They ran three regimes, up to an 881M model on 50B tokens. With scarce text, repeating a 100M-token corpus beat every synthetic curriculum. Earlier reported gains were sensitive to weight initialisation. Significance: it undercuts the idea that a knowledge-free reasoning stage before language gives a model a head start. (arXiv)

RL taught a model to learn from a textbook on the fly, raising proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% (arXiv:2610.39671). The model navigates a textbook through a tool, studies the relevant principles and then proves the exercises, with reference solutions withheld. It beat RL baselines that had no textbook or only fixed retrieval, on both in-domain and out-of-domain books. Significance: it is direct evidence for reasoning that acquires knowledge at test time rather than storing it. (arXiv)

A small transformer's failure to compose unseen facts was fixed by changing how the facts were presented in training (arXiv:2610.39016). In a controlled two-hop task, a GPT-2-like model composed facts it had seen composed but failed on held-out facts. Showing the atomic facts in the position the second-hop circuit reads from closed the gap. No architecture change or extra compositional supervision was needed. Significance: one compositionality gap is a data-layout problem, not a capacity limit, which supports keeping knowledge and reasoning separable. (arXiv)
