Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-10-04

Market Overview

It is Sunday, so no major market traded since yesterday's digest. US stocks finished last week roughly flat. Friday's rally after the weak jobs report only made up for earlier losses. The new week opens with an OPEC+ meeting today and the Fed's minutes later in the week. Mainland China stays shut for Golden Week.

Financial News

The S&P 500 ended the week roughly flat and is up 12.8% for the year. The Nasdaq finished the week higher, while the Dow and the S&P 500 slipped. The equal-weight S&P 500 is up 9.5% for the year, so large tech stocks are still doing most of the lifting. (Advisor Perspectives, VistaP Global)

Gold fell for a second straight week. It closed Friday near $4,140 an ounce, down about 2.4% on the week and 7.5% over the month. Higher oil, a firmer dollar and high Treasury yields all weighed on it. (Trading Economics, Yahoo Finance)

OPEC+ meets online today to set November output targets. It is expected to leave them unchanged, as it did for October. Actual output has been running below the official targets, so a rollover says little about how many barrels reach the market. (TokenPost, Energy Connects)

The coming week brings the Fed's minutes, a 10-year auction and early earnings. The September ISM services survey is out on Monday, and the Treasury sells $39 billion of 10-year notes on Tuesday. PepsiCo reports on Thursday and Delta Air Lines on Friday. The main earnings season starts on 13 October. (CNBC, Investrade)

Mainland China is closed for Golden Week until 7 October. Hong Kong and the offshore yuan carry the China signal until then. The World Gold Council says the holiday usually starts China's peak season for gold jewellery buying, but high prices and weak consumer confidence have held demand back this year. (World Gold Council, MUFG Research)

Bitcoin traded around $84,900 on Saturday afternoon US time. That was up about 0.5% on the day, on thin weekend volume of about $6 billion. (CoinDesk)

Tech News

Apple says it will tighten macOS Full Disk Access because of AI agents. Apple said always-on agents make broad access to files, mail, messages and browsing history riskier. Granting it will need "very explicit user action". Apple gave no date. The move came days after a journalist said Meta's Muse app read their private messages, which Meta disputes. (TechCrunch, MacRumors)

Kagi is ending Orion for Linux and Windows and will open-source both. The Linux beta stopped getting updates on 2 October, and the planned Windows release will not come from Kagi. The company will focus on Orion for macOS and iOS. (Kagi, HN thread)

A federal judge blocked Utah's law requiring adult sites to block VPN users. SB 73 required sites to find each visitor's physical location or verify every visitor's age. The court found that no technology can do that reliably. Aylo, which runs Pornhub, brought the suit, and the EFF welcomed the ruling. (EFF, HN thread)

Cloudflare opened a closed beta of its OHTTP Gateway. It lets a site behind Cloudflare accept Oblivious HTTP requests, so the backend never learns the user's IP address. Cloudflare also renamed its Privacy Gateway to OHTTP Relay. (Cloudflare, HN thread)

System76's COSMIC desktop now rejects pull requests containing any LLM-generated content. The ban covers code, comments and descriptions. System76's Jeremy Soller said AI-written submissions were swamping maintainers and were rarely accepted. Non-generative tools such as bug finders are still allowed. (XDA, HN thread)

Microsoft began rolling out Windows 11 version 26H2. It ships as a small enablement package on the same codebase as 24H2 and 25H2. The main gains are a fresh support clock and automatic cloud backup of settings and some files. (BleepingComputer, Thurrott)

Apple released Pass Designer, a Mac app for building Apple Wallet passes, in beta. It previews a pass on iPhone and Apple Watch as you edit and flags missing required fields. It needs macOS 27. (Apple, HN thread)

AI Papers & Discussions

Aleph Alpha released Kolibri, an open-weight German and English model under Apache 2.0. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 78 billion parameters, of which about 3.5 billion are active per token, and a 1-million-token context. It was trained from scratch on 24 trillion tokens, all in Germany and Finland. It is built for regulated public-sector work and is trained to decline when its context lacks the evidence for an answer. (Aleph Alpha, tech report, HN thread)

RL post-training makes agents more reliable but solve fewer distinct tasks (arXiv:2610.01509). Across 14 base and post-trained model pairs, base models with a light harness often solved more tasks given many attempts. Post-training pushes each task towards "always solved" or "never solved". The authors call the lost coverage the "sharpening tax" and propose a sampler that pays less of it. (arXiv)

Much of a small model's failure as an agent comes from the harness, not the model (arXiv:2610.02001). Mingbird is a local harness for 2B to 9B models with ten fixes, such as re-reading the task before accepting "done". On 18 real tasks it scored 0.886 against 0.631 for goose and 0.479 for opencode. The authors flag the limits: they built the benchmark, ran it on one machine, and scored each task once. (arXiv)

Debate and other oversight protocols cannot in general verify an answer without leaking the private data behind it (arXiv:2610.01995). The authors, including Alessandro Chiesa, prove that general oracle-aided computation has no zero-knowledge proofs, and the result extends to debate. If the oracle signs each answer, every such computation can be verified in zero knowledge. That also gives a route to scalable oversight that needs no honest opponent. (arXiv)

A human audit of WebArena-Lite found the automatic grader misses 5 to 8 points of real successes (arXiv:2610.01491). The authors re-checked all 165 tasks by hand across four GPT-5.5 settings. They also read 102 failed runs. The common failures were scrolling loops, giving up early, and half-finished forms. Leaderboard scores on this benchmark understate agents and say little about how they fail. (arXiv)
